\begin{abstract}
This document consolidates every retrieval, reranking, and generation experiment run
for the hypertension-guideline RAG system on the 200-question benchmark set. Every table
reports the metric values that were computed and stored on disk, and each table states
the exact path of its output JSON file. A dedicated section explains the meaning and
mathematical definition of every reported metric. The production configuration derived
from these results is sparse BM25 on the deduplicated corpus ($k=5$), with generation by
Gemini Flash Lite at temperature~0.1.
\end{abstract}

\section{Corpus and Test Sets}

\textbf{Corpus.} Four guideline documents: WHO 2021 pharmacological treatment guideline,
2024 ESC guideline, USPSTF screening recommendation, and CDC Health E-Stats.

\textbf{Test set (200 questions).} All evaluation reported in this document uses the
200-question benchmark set (WHO 60 / ESC 90 / USPSTF 30 / CDC 20), stored as:

\src{evaluation/benchmark\_200.py} (\texttt{--corpus original|dedup}).

\section{Evaluation Metrics and Their Meaning}

This section defines every metric reported in the tables below.

\subsection{Hit Rate @ $k$ (hit@$k$)}

\textbf{Meaning:} the fraction of queries for which at least one \emph{expected} chunk
(or a chunk from the expected section) appears among the top-$k$ retrieved chunks. It is
a \emph{recall}-type measure of how often the ground-truth evidence is captured.

\[
\text{hit@}k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=1}^{N} \mathbb{1}\big[\text{expected}(q) \in \text{top-}k(q)\big]
\]

where $N$ is the number of queries, $\mathbb{1}[\cdot]$ is the indicator function (1 if
the condition holds, 0 otherwise), and $\text{top-}k(q)$ is the set of the $k$ chunks
returned for query $q$. Higher is better.

\subsection{Precision @ $k$ (P@$k$)}

\textbf{Meaning:} the average fraction of the top-$k$ retrieved chunks that are relevant
(i.e.\ belong to the expected set). It measures how \emph{noisy} the retrieved context is;
high precision means the prompt context is tightly focused on the answer.

\[
\text{P@}k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=1}^{N} \frac{\big|\text{relevant}(q) \cap \text{top-}k(q)\big|}{k}
\]

Higher is better, but precision and recall trade off against each other as $k$ grows
(including more chunks raises hit@$k$ but lowers P@$k$).

\subsection{Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)}

\textbf{Meaning:} the average of the reciprocal of the rank at which the first expected
chunk appears. It rewards retrievers that place the correct evidence at the very top.

\[
\text{MRR} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=1}^{N} \frac{1}{\text{rank}\big(\text{first expected}(q)\big)}
\]

where $\text{rank}(\cdot)$ is 1 if the first expected chunk is the top result, 2 if it is
second, and so on; if no expected chunk is retrieved, the term is 0. Range $[0, 1]$,
higher is better.

\subsection{Average Query Latency (ms)}

\textbf{Meaning:} the mean wall-clock time taken by the retriever to answer a single query
(including embedding API calls where applicable). Reported in milliseconds; lower is
better. It captures the cost of the method (e.g.\ a cross-encoder reranker is far slower
than plain BM25).

\subsection{Correctness (0--5)}

\textbf{Meaning:} an LLM judge (temperature 0.0) rates each generated answer against the
expected answer on a 0--5 rubric, where 5 means the answer is fully correct and complete
with respect to the guideline, and lower scores indicate partially correct, incomplete,
or wrong answers. It measures \emph{end-to-end answer quality} (retrieval + generation).

\subsection{Faithfulness (0--5)}

\textbf{Meaning:} an LLM judge rates whether every statement in the generated answer is
\emph{supported by the retrieved context}. 5 means the answer is fully grounded in the
sources with no hallucination; lower scores indicate statements invented or contradicted
by the context. It measures \emph{grounding}, not factual completeness.

\subsection{Citation Validity (0--1)}

\textbf{Meaning:} the fraction of answers whose inline citations were judged valid
against the retrieved sources by the LLM judge. 1.0 means every answer cited its sources
correctly. Separately, a rule-based checker counts citations found / auto-valid /
auto-invalid; discrepancies are mostly checker false-positives (child subsection cited
vs parent section title retrieved).

\section{Why Top-$k = 5$? (Retrieval Depth Justification)}

The production retrieval depth ($k=5$) is not the value suggested by a naive reading of
the early pilot data, so this section documents the decision explicitly.

\subsection{Initial pilot (10-question set) --- ambiguous}

A first tuning pass (\src{evaluation/tune\_k.py} $\rightarrow$
\src{evaluation/top\_k\_tuning\_results.json}) tested $k \in \{1,2,3,4,5,8,10\}$ on a
10-question WHO pilot set using dense retrieval. There, hit rate saturated at 100\% from
$k=2$, while precision fell as $k$ grew, so the pilot alone would have suggested
$k \in \{2,3\}$ (its own weighted score
$\text{tradeoff} = 0.6\cdot\text{hit} + 0.4\cdot\text{precision}$ peaks at $k=2$).
This pilot is \emph{not} the decision basis: only 10 questions, one document, dense-only,
and trivially saturating recall.

\subsection{200-question benchmark --- decisive}

At the 200-question scale, recall does \textbf{not} saturate at $k=3$. The sparse BM25
numbers from Tables~\ref{tab:orig} and \ref{tab:dedup} give:

Going from $k=3$ to $k=5$ recovers an additional \textbf{+11.0\,pts} of recall on the
original corpus and \textbf{+5.5\,pts} on the production (dedup) corpus. That is, at
$k=3$ roughly 11 more of the 200 questions would arrive at the generator without their
evidence chunk in context. The marginal cost is two extra chunks ($\approx$1,200
tokens), a small price for a $\approx$5--11\% recall gain; beyond $k=5$ the precision@$k$
drops sharply (62.5\% $\rightarrow$ 54\% $\rightarrow$ 35\% at $k=4/5/8$) with almost no
additional recall.

A $k$ below 5 also risks missing multi-part recommendations (drug choice plus
contraindications plus comorbidity guidance live in different chunks), while $k > 5$
dilutes the prompt context and increases cost. $k=5$ is therefore the largest retrieval
depth before marginal recall gain becomes negligible; on the dedup corpus $\approx$92\%
of correct chunks are inside BM25 top-5. The residual misses (e.g.\ Q019, Q084, Q095)
are not even in top-10, so no $k$ recovers them --- they are the queries for which the
model correctly refuses rather than fabricates.

\textbf{Findings.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Perfect grounding: zero hallucination across all 200 answers (faithfulness 5.0/5).
  \item All low-correctness cases are \emph{retrieval} gaps (correct chunk absent from

  \item The 47 auto-checked ``invalid'' citations are mostly checker false-positives
        (child subsection cited vs parent section title retrieved; page offsets); the LLM
        judge scored 0.99.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item BM25 on the dedup corpus is the production retriever: 75.5\% hit@1, MRR 0.826,
        $\approx$2\,ms/query.
  \item Deduplication (813$\rightarrow$435) was the single biggest lever (+17.5\,pts hit@1).
  \item No dense, hybrid, or reranker variant beats plain BM25 on this corpus (2 embedding
        models, 3 cross-encoders, 6 fused/rerank combos tested).
  \item End-to-end quality is strong: faithfulness 5.0/5, correctness 4.84/5; remaining
        errors trace to retrieval misses ($\approx$11 queries), where the model correctly
        refuses.
\end{enumerate}
